\section{Forward PINNs}

\paragraph{Scope and labels.} All trajectories are \emph{synthetic} and dimensionless
($x=N/K$, $\tau=rt$); no species-specific claim is made. The question is how well a small
neural network that is given only the ODE and the initial condition reproduces the
population, compared with a standard solver.

\subsection{Method}
For Model A, $\dot x=G(x)-q$, and Model B, $\dot x=G(x)-ex$, with $G(x)=x(1-x)(x/a-1)$ and
$\dot x=dx/d\tau$, write $\dot x=f(x)$. The network is a fully connected $\tanh$ network with layer sizes
$(1,32,32,1)$ in float64 (JAX). The initial condition is enforced \emph{exactly} by the hard embedding
\begin{equation}
\hat x(\tau)=x_0+s\,N(s;\theta),\qquad s=\tau/T,
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the network and $T$ a fixed constant, so $\hat x(0)=x_0$ for any weights $\theta$ and no
initial-condition loss is needed. Automatic differentiation with respect to the physical time $\tau$ applies the chain
rule $d\hat x/d\tau=T^{-1}\,d\hat x/ds$ (including the product rule on $s\,N(s)$). The residual and the only loss are
\begin{equation}
R(\tau)=\frac{d\hat x}{d\tau}-f(\hat x),\qquad
\mathcal L(\theta)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n R(\tau_i)^2 ,
\end{equation}
with $n=100$ collocation points, a fixed uniform grid on $[0,T]$ including both endpoints.
Training uses 3500 iterations: Adam (1000 iterations, rate $10^{-2}$, about $2/7$ of the budget) to move into a good region,
then L-BFGS-B (2500 iterations, SciPy, using the exact JAX gradient) to converge. No solver
solution is used as a label. For Model A, $T$ is the extinction time of the reference solution
(a domain endpoint only), so the original residual is never evaluated on a post-extinction zero tail.
For Model B, $T=10$.

\subsection{Evaluation}
Predictions are compared on a separate dense grid of 1001 points with DOP853
($\mathrm{rtol}=10^{-12}$, $\mathrm{atol}=10^{-14}$), not used in training. We report RMSE, MAE, global relative
$L^2$ error $\|\hat x-x_{\rm ref}\|_2/\|x_{\rm ref}\|_2$ (always given in scientific notation, never as a percentage),
the maximum absolute error, the number of negative predicted samples, and wall-clock time.
A run is labelled \emph{inaccurate} if its relative $L^2$ error exceeds $5\times10^{-2}$, a threshold fixed
before the final run. The classical baseline is the project RK45 wrapper, scored on the same grid.

\subsection{Results (3 seeds: 11, 22, 33; 18 PINN runs)}
Relative $L^2$ errors by case (seeds 11, 22, 33):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
Case & seed 11 & seed 22 & seed 33\\\hline
A regular & $7.6\times10^{-1}$ & $1.9\times10^{-5}$ & $8.5\times10^{-1}$\\
A near-fold & $9.1\times10^{-2}$ & $6.0\times10^{-3}$ & $4.2\times10^{-4}$\\
A subthreshold extinction & $8.3\times10^{-7}$ & $1.2\times10^{-7}$ & $1.9\times10^{-7}$\\
B regular & $7.3\times10^{-1}$ & $6.8\times10^{-1}$ & $9.2\times10^{-1}$\\
B near-fold & $9.3\times10^{-8}$ & $5.5\times10^{-7}$ & $8.5\times10^{-7}$\\
B subthreshold decay & $1.0\times10^{-4}$ & $8.2\times10^{-5}$ & $3.2\times10^{-5}$\\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Twelve runs met the accuracy threshold and six did not; none produced a non-finite loss.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Accurate cases.} A subthreshold extinction, B near-fold and B subthreshold decay reach
$10^{-7}$--$10^{-4}$ for every seed. B subthreshold decay has 68 and 69 slightly negative predicted samples
(seeds 11, 22; largest error $4.9\times10^{-6}$) near the zero limit, and A extinction seed 11 has one.
\item \textbf{B regular fails for all three seeds} ($6.8\times10^{-1}$--$9.2\times10^{-1}$)
\emph{despite training losses of $2\times10^{-8}$--$3\times10^{-6}$}. Seeds 11 and 22 leave $x_0=0.8$ within the first
collocation interval and settle at $0.2837$ and $0.2838$, the unstable equilibrium $x_-=0.28377$ where $f=0$ and so $R=0$.
Seed 33 decays toward the equilibrium $x=0$. These are spurious zero-residual solutions that a small loss cannot distinguish
from the true one. The hard initial condition fixes $\hat x(0)$ but does not prevent this.
\item \textbf{A regular} fails for seeds 11 and 33 and succeeds for seed 22. Seed 11 jumps to
$\approx0.30$ near the unstable root $x_-=0.29598$ and later drifts negative; seed 33 has not converged
(final loss $5.8\times10^{-2}$).
\item \textbf{A near-fold} (bottleneck then collapse) is inaccurate for seed 11 ($9.1\times10^{-2}$) and accurate for the others.
\end{itemize}
The L-BFGS phase is decisive: the loss typically sits on a plateau during Adam and drops by several orders of magnitude only
after L-BFGS takes over, and for the failed seeds it often escapes the plateau only at a spurious solution.
Every PINN run took $2.4$--$3.6$ s (including compilation), whereas RK45 took $3\times10^{-3}$--$9\times10^{-3}$ s with relative $L^2$ error of
order $10^{-10}$--$10^{-9}$. On this scalar ODE the conventional solver is faster and more accurate by many orders
of magnitude, so the value of the PINN here is as a method to be understood and as the engine for the inverse problems
(Task 5), not as a better solver.

\subsection{Limitations}
One parameter set ($a=0.2$) per regime; one hyperparameter configuration, deliberately not tuned on the
reported results; three seeds only, and the failures are strongly seed dependent; CPU timings from one machine (see manifest).
With only $n=100$ collocation points a fast initial transient can fit between two points, which is consistent with, but was not
separately shown to be the cause of, the observed jumps to equilibria. Remedies (denser early collocation, causal time-marching, more seeds) are
untested and are \emph{not} claimed to work.
